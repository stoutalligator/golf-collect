# Golf App — Asset & Visual Exploration Brief

*Last updated: November 25, 2026*
*Status: Exploration brief; concepts are not final specifications*

## 1. Creative direction

Create a distinctive visual identity for a **premium, modern golf collection**.

The overall direction blends:

- **Modern iOS product design:** clean, clear, polished, highly usable.
- **Golf heritage:** scorecards, club insignias, traditional golf ephemera, understated craftsmanship.
- **Golf exploration:** destination courses, travel, discovery, regional collections, and memorable places.
- **Collecting:** physical-feeling keepsakes, course-specific objects, tiers, and display-worthy achievements.

The result should have charm and a recognizable point of view. Avoid generic golf-app visuals, overly sterile dashboard styling, excessive country-club formality, or making every asset look old/vintage.

## 3. Asset families to explore

### A. Brand identity

- Primary logo / wordmark.
- Compact emblem or monogram.
- iOS app icon.
- Small navigation and profile marks.
- Optional course-collection stamp or seal.

Explore an emblem that can work as a small app icon and as a mark stamped onto scorecards or collectibles. Avoid locking into a golf ball, flag, or crossed-clubs cliché without exploring more distinctive alternatives.

### B. Course collectibles

The collection should support multiple collectible formats. **Not every course needs to use a ball marker.**

Potential formats:

1. **Classic ball marker:** circular metal/enamel token for everyday courses.
2. **Course crest or badge:** shield, patch, or embroidered-style emblem.
3. **Destination medallion:** more elaborate commemorative piece for bucket-list courses.
4. **Illustrated course tile:** small framed landscape or signature-hole illustration.
5. **Ticket / passport stamp:** travel-inspired collectible for regional or course-completion collections.
6. **Special edition artifact:** limited-style design for notable milestones, events, or particularly iconic courses.

A course's identity, character, and significance can influence the collectible format. The system should still feel cohesive across all formats.

### C. Collectible tiers and variants

Explore a tier system such as:

- Bronze
- Silver
- Gold
- Platinum / special edition

Tier treatments might use material, border, engraving, foil, or subtle visual details. Do not rely on color alone to communicate tier.

Potential earning dimensions:

- First visit / first completed round.
- Performance at that course.
- Personal best at that course.
- Repeat visits or course-specific milestones.
- Special event or tournament participation.

Thresholds and eligibility rules are undecided. Visual concepts should be designed so tiers can be shown without implying that every course or collectible must have identical rules.

### D. Badges and achievements

Create a flexible family of badges for:

- Score milestones (breaking 100, 90, 80, etc.).
- Birdies and eagles.
- Career round milestones.
- Courses visited.
- Regional course collections.
- Streaks or seasonal challenges.
- League and tournament accomplishments.
- Special or rare achievements.

Visual approaches to test:

- Enamel pin.
- Embroidered patch.
- Engraved metal medallion.
- Heritage stamp.
- Minimal modern emblem with a subtle vintage frame.

Badges should be recognizable at small sizes and look good together as a collection.

### E. Scorecard and round keepsakes

Explore a digital scorecard that feels inspired by a physical course scorecard without sacrificing mobile usability.

Possible elements:

- Course logo or emblem.
- Course name and location.
- Date and players.
- Tee selection and course details.
- Hole-by-hole score grid.
- Front nine, back nine, and total.
- Selected round statistics.
- A subtle paper, print, or ticket-like treatment.
- A shareable completed-round view.

Create both an in-round score-entry treatment (fast and legible) and a completed scorecard treatment (more expressive and keepsake-like). They do not need to be identical screens.

### F. Course artwork and imagery

Explore:

- Signature-hole illustrations.
- Course landscape thumbnails.
- Destination-course hero artwork.
- Regional collection artwork.
- Course-specific color or motif accents.

Possible illustration styles:

- Refined painterly landscape.
- Vintage travel-poster illustration.
- Engraving / line-art inspired course illustration.
- Modern editorial illustration.

Course logos and photographs should only be used where rights and licensing permit. Where official artwork is unavailable, the app may need a consistent original illustration approach.

### G. UI components and surfaces

Explore a cohesive visual system for:

- Course cards.
- Collectible cards and detail views.
- Achievement grids.
- Profile statistics.
- Round history.
- Friends' shared-round cards.
- League and tournament standings.
- Search results and course details.
- GPS hole view as a future module.

The core app should stay clean and modern. Reserve richer textures, ornamental borders, and heritage-inspired detail for moments where they add meaning.

## 4. Specific concept mockups to create

Suggested first exploration batch:

1. **Brand board:** wordmark directions, emblem, palette, typography, and visual motifs.
2. **App icon sheet:** several distinct icon concepts that remain legible at small sizes.
3. **Course collectible family:** one everyday local course ball marker, one course crest, and one destination-course medallion.
4. **Tier comparison:** the same collectible shown in bronze, silver, gold, and platinum treatments.
5. **Achievement badge sheet:** a varied set of score, birdie, course-count, and regional badges.
6. **Physical-inspired scorecard:** one in-round score-entry screen and one completed keepsake scorecard.
7. **Course detail / collection page:** course identity, personal stats, rounds played, and collectible.
8. **Profile dashboard:** golf performance and course-collection progress in one coherent visual language.

## 5. Example collectible scenarios

Use fictional or clearly labeled sample courses for early mockups unless real course branding is approved.

- **Everyday local course:** a simple, handsome ball-marker collectible.
- **Distinctive regional course:** a course crest or illustrated badge that reflects its landscape.
- **Bucket-list destination (e.g., Pebble Beach):** a special destination medallion or illustrated keepsake, visually more elaborate than a standard marker.
- **Regional completion:** a passport stamp or collection emblem for completing a state or region.

The point is to make the collectible format responsive to the course's identity and significance while keeping the overall collection visually unified.

## 6. Design principles and guardrails

- Premium, not flashy.
- Modern, not sterile.
- Heritage-inspired, not dated.
- Distinctive, not generic.
- Collectible, not cluttered.
- Cohesive, but allow different course collectibles to have their own personality.
- Usable during a round: legibility and speed take priority over decoration.
- Accessible contrast and readable type sizes.
- Do not use metallic effects, shadows, textures, or ornamental frames everywhere.
- Do not make every course collectible a ball marker.
- Avoid assuming all courses have usable official logos or photography.

## 7. Open questions for future design sessions

- What name and brand story should the app have?
- Should the core symbol be an emblem, monogram, mascot, or abstract mark?
- Which palette works best in actual screens, not just a brand board?
- How much serif typography feels right?
- Which illustration style is distinctive but scalable?
- How should destination collectibles differ from everyday course collectibles?
- What makes a collectible feel earned and worth displaying?
- How should dark mode work?
- Which assets need to be static, and which should have subtle animation?

## 8. Suggested production order

1. Brand moodboard and three logo/emblem directions.
2. Palette and typography studies applied to sample UI.
3. App icon concepts.
4. Course collectible formats and tier treatments.
5. Achievement badge family.
6. Scorecard and course detail mockups.
7. Profile/dashboard mockup.
8. Refine a reusable asset specification once the direction is selected.

## One-sentence creative brief

*Design a modern golf companion with the polish of a premium iOS app and the soul of a golf travel collection: a place where courses, rounds, and achievements become keepsakes worth revisiting.*
